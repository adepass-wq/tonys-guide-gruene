Why make room for it?

JTB describes the museum and Asafu Yard as places where Charles Town Maroons share history and cultural practices. Present-day hosts are the authority on what is appropriate for the visit. An account of a past tour is not a guarantee that drumming, food or a forest walk is included today.

Look, listen and ask

Ask how community memory, written records and continuing practice relate to one another. Request permission before recording speech, music or ritual material. Continue with the Moore Town and Maroon history pathways rather than treating every Maroon community as interchangeable.

Tony’s call
Arrange the visit with the community and let hosts explain what can be shared.
Best for
Visitors ready to listen to Maroon perspectives on history, music and community life.
Skip if
You expect unrestricted photography, a fixed ceremony or an unbooked performance.
Time — planning estimate
Allow a half-day including transfer as an estimate; agree the actual programme with hosts.
Cost
Ask about the chosen museum/community visit, food, guiding and any separately arranged activity.
Hours & booking
Confirm the date, host, meeting point and programme; festival dates require their own announcement.
Access
Ask about museum entry, yard surfaces, seating, toilets and whether a shorter visit is possible.
Parking & pickup
Arrange the exact entrance and pickup with your host, driver or venue; directions are destination-based, not a surveyed route.
Next stop
Choose a separate Rio Grande day unless your host and driver confirm a sensible combination.
