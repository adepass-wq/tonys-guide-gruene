Seville Heritage Park

The strongest starting point for connecting Taíno settlement, colonial property, enslavement and the people who lived on this coast.

Directions ↗
Read Tony’s guide & practical details

Take the guided interpretation seriously, then spend time with the grounds. The operator’s museum tour includes reconstructed Taíno and enslaved African dwellings; those are reconstructions, not surviving homes of named ancestors. Read our Seville household feature before going so the great house does not dominate the whole visit. This is a history stop with a landscape, not a beach attraction with a historical plaque.

Tony’s Call Make Seville the anchor of a weekday heritage day. Its own tour page says normal tours are closed on weekends and public holidays; a special event may have separate arrangements.
Best for
Jamaicans, diaspora researchers, curious families and visitors wanting context.
Skip if
Your only available day is a weekend or holiday without a confirmed special opening.
Timing
Official tour approximately 45 minutes; allow 1½–2 hours overall.
Cost
Operator booking page lists US$15 adult and US$6 child. Ask about local rates and group arrangements; confirm the rate at booking.
Hours & booking
Normal group-tour booking: Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.; closed weekends/public holidays according to the operator.
Accessibility
Grounds and museum routes may differ. Request step counts, surface information and restroom access; full step-free access is not verified.
Parking & arrival
Arrange the visitor entrance and parking in advance; do not treat archaeological grounds as unrestricted picnic space.
Sensible next stop
St. Ann’s Bay, then Scotchies at Drax Hall.
