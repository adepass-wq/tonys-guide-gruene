Requests for money, persistent selling & unwanted help

A person asking for help is not automatically dishonest or dangerous. You can treat people respectfully and still decline. You do not have to explain your finances, buy a souvenir, accept a guide, or pay for a service you never requested.

Decline before anything starts

Use one calm sentence: “No thank you, I’m not buying today.” For an unsolicited escort: “I don’t need a guide. Please don’t follow me.” Keep walking toward your planned destination rather than entering a prolonged negotiation.

Agree the whole price

Before a photograph, braid, raft ride, parking assistance or tour, ask: “What is the total, in Jamaican dollars or US dollars, including all extras?” Confirm the duration and what is included. If “free” later becomes a fee, stop the activity and seek staff help.

Tips are your decision

Check the bill for an existing service charge and ask how it is applied. Decide any extra tip according to the actual service and your budget. “I’ve paid the agreed amount; that is my final payment” is enough. Do not keep adding money because the demand grows louder.

When pressure becomes threatening

If someone blocks you, touches you, follows you after a clear refusal or threatens you, move toward staffed premises or security. Avoid insults, filming a confrontation or bargaining in an isolated place. Ask a named staff member to help you leave. In a robbery, prioritize your safety over property.

If you wish to give, choose an amount privately; avoid displaying your wallet or a roll of cash. You can also support a community organisation you have independently checked. Do not hand your phone or passport to someone offering unsolicited assistance.

A quick scam check before you pay

  • Bookings: open the attraction or accommodation’s own website yourself. Confirm the address, cancellation terms and recipient before a deposit; a social account or polished message alone does not establish a genuine business.
  • Changed destination: if a driver says your hotel or attraction is closed and suggests an alternative, call your original booking directly. Do not accept a replacement simply because someone claims to work there.
  • Unclear currencies: ask whether a price is JMD or USD. Check the final card-terminal amount and currency before approving it; keep receipts.
  • Uninvited services: do not let someone start carrying bags, guiding you or taking photographs while the charge is undecided. Use the venue’s official desk.
  • ATM help: cover your PIN and decline strangers’ assistance. If a card is retained, contact your bank using its official number rather than one supplied by a bystander.
  • Urgent requests: slow down when asked to pay immediately, disclose a banking code, send money to an unrelated person or bypass the normal checkout.

Choose transport before you set off

For an unfamiliar route, Tony’s practical preference is a reputable, prearranged driver or transfer that your hotel or operator can identify. This gives you a clear pickup, price and return arrangement; it is not a guarantee against every risk.

Airport arrival

Reserve through a hotel, recognised operator or official airport transport desk. Check the driver’s name and company against your booking before loading bags. Do not follow someone solely because they are holding a sign. Confirm your destination and return contact independently.

Taxis & private drivers

FCDO recommends JTB-approved taxis and minibuses; hotel-assigned drivers typically carry photo ID and a blue JTB windscreen sticker. Verify the booking as well as the vehicle. Agree the total fare, currency, stops, waiting time and return pickup before departure. Official taxi guidance ↗

Public & shared transport

Ask a trusted local host to explain the route, terminus and fare before using a route taxi or bus. A shared route service is different from a private charter. Avoid improvising a complicated transfer with luggage or a tight return deadline. FCDO advises avoiding buses at night. Transport risks ↗

Rental cars & long drives

Jamaica drives on the left. Roads may be winding, poorly lit or damaged; plan daylight travel and check weather and closures. Choose a licensed rental company, inspect tyres and lights, and confirm insurance, spare tyre and child seats. Use seatbelts; avoid an unfamiliar shortcut just because a map estimates it is faster. Your pre-departure checklist

Send a trusted person the driver/company, destination and expected return; charge your phone, keep an offline address and agree a staffed pickup point. Ask your host about the actual route and current conditions. Leave enough daylight for the return. If plans change, tell your contact. Verify each attraction’s opening before undertaking a long cross-island drive.

At Norman Manley airport, ask your operator about the recommended route into Kingston. FCDO identifies security concerns on the Mountain View route and describes the Humming Bird route via South Camp Road as the usual risk-reducing alternative. Airport route guidance ↗

If you need help

Move to a staffed hotel, attraction or business and ask for security or police assistance. If a crime occurs, request an incident reference, contact your insurer and use your embassy’s official contact page. Do not chase a thief or resist an armed robbery.
