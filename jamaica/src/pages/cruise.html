Before you leave the ship

01 · THE CLOCK

Write down all-aboard

Use the time announced by your ship. Check whether ship time matches Jamaica time; your phone changing its clock is not a boarding instruction. Record the deadline, ship emergency contact and the pier’s name.

02 · THE PIER

Confirm where you docked

Ocho Rios has the main cruise terminal and the separate Reynolds Pier. Ask the port desk which you are using and how to return to it. A guide to the town terminal is not automatically a walking route from Reynolds.

03 · THE RIDE

Check the driver and the whole price

Use the official port transport desk, a ship excursion or a reputable operator you can identify. Match the driver to your booking. Agree currency, number of passengers, stops, waiting, return pickup and the total before leaving.

04 · THE DAY

Choose an anchor

One principal attraction, a meal and a short shopping stop usually make a better port plan than several distant excursions. Book the actual admission or activity, and ask what happens if weather stops it.

Three ways to spend the day

TOWN · LOW TRANSFER COMMITMENT

Food, a park and shopping

Start with Island Village or the town centre after confirming your pier’s route. Pick a patty or a sit-down meal, visit Turtle River Park and choose the craft market or one shopping centre. Add the town beach only after checking entry and your return margin.

ONE MAIN OUTING · PREARRANGED RETURN

A waterfall or river experience

Choose Dunn’s River, a Blue Hole visit, or White River rafting rather than trying to do all three. Ask about physical demands, life jackets, changing facilities and exact inclusions. Keep the return driver and pickup point settled before the activity.

Shop with a destination and a budget

Crafts and gifts

Ask who made the item and what materials it uses. Compare quality and prices, agree a currency and get a receipt. Ask before photographing a vendor. A pleasant conversation does not oblige you to purchase.

Coffee, rum and packaged food

Read the label, size and seal before paying. Confirm what your ship allows onboard and your home country’s import rules. Keep liquids and fragile items protected; a bargain is less useful if it cannot travel with you.

Jewellery and larger purchases

Request a written description, final price and return policy. Confirm the seller’s identity independently. Avoid letting urgency, “ship approval” claims or a promised free gift replace your own checks.

Money

Ask “Jamaican dollars or US dollars?” before agreeing a price. Check the card-terminal amount and currency before authorising it. Carry modest cash for the day and avoid displaying your wallet or accepting a stranger’s ATM assistance.

Be friendly. Keep your boundaries.

Start respectfully

A greeting—“Good morning” or “Good afternoon”—sets a better tone than treating everyone as a potential transaction. Ask questions normally; do not perform a mock accent. People are working, commuting and living here while you are visiting.

Active selling

Use “No thank you, I’m not buying today.” Decline a guide, photograph or bag-carrying service before it begins if you do not want it. If you do want a service, agree the full charge first.

Requests for money and extra tips

You may decline without discussing your finances. Check whether your bill already includes service. For a growing demand, say “I’ve paid the agreed amount; that is my final payment.” Move towards staffed premises if someone follows, blocks or threatens you.

Common checks that prevent problems

Call your original operator if someone says your booking is closed or cancelled. Do not follow an uninvited escort to another vendor. Keep your room number, ship cabin and banking codes private. Ask port staff for help rather than arguing in an isolated spot.

Before you turn back towards the ship

  • Know which pier and gate you are returning to.
  • Leave by the deadline you calculated, rather than your activity’s advertised finish.
  • Keep the driver’s contact, a backup transport plan and the ship contact available offline.
  • Check purchases, belongings and the agreed bill before the final departure.
