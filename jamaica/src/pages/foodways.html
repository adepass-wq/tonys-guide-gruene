Variation is part of the evidence

Rundown: look at the fish and the coconut

Lesa’s mackerel rundown and her saltfish rundown document different fish preparations. Her coconut prawns are explicitly described as rundown-inspired. Keep that distinction when cooking or ordering.

One fritter, more than one name

Joan Lue’s stamp and go identifies the dish as saltfish fritters. Two names in a catalogue do not establish two separate histories. Her accompanying origin explanation remains a claim requiring independent historical evidence.

Turn cornmeal and its Caribbean relatives

Sian Rose’s corned-pork version is attached to her own cooking experience. Charla’s cou-cou comparison distinguishes her Trinidadian okra preparation from Jamaican turn cornmeal. Shared ingredients do not erase those identities.

A modern adaptation can still be worth cooking

Lesa’s chickpea curry is explicitly Jamaican-and-Indian fusion. Original Flava’s Ital rundown is their interpretation inspired by Rastafari cooks. Attribution makes the adaptation intelligible without inventing an inherited lineage.

EDITORIAL STANDARDS

What “documented” means here

  1. Identify the preparation. Name the cook or publisher and locate the actual dish.
  2. Read the recipe. Check that quantities and a preparation are supplied. Record omissions, contradictions and adaptations.
  3. Keep history separate. A contemporary recipe does not establish an inventor, parish origin or colonial date.
  4. Test before claiming a tested formula. Record ingredients, yield, equipment, temperatures, texture and results. No Tony’s Guide kitchen trials have been recorded.
Ingredients and safety: where a recipe needs more than tradition

Published recipes sometimes omit safety detail. The FDA distinguishes ripe, properly processed ackee from poisonous unripe fruit, rind and seeds. The cassava entries here document practice without supplying a validated detoxification procedure for an unidentified variety.

For dried kidney beans, follow verified soaking and boiling guidance rather than assuming a slow-cooker stew makes raw beans safe. EFSA’s lectin guidance explains the concern. For poultry, USDA specifies 165°F / 74°C measured with a thermometer. No preserve or bottled sauce in this collection has a Tony’s Guide validated shelf-stable process.

No external recipe photographs or expressive instructions have been copied into this directory. Three local source transcriptions retain published ingredient measurements with paraphrased directions. Two additional transcriptions are withheld because the sources need clarification. Follow the publisher’s link to examine the original preparation. Family and parish testimony requires an identified speaker, location, recording date and permission before being presented as such.
