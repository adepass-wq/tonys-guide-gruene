AUDIO · BROADCAST INTERVIEW · 10 OCTOBER 1940

Una Marson asks the questions

Who and where: Jamaican-born Una Marson interviews Ken “Snakehips” Johnson for the BBC Empire Service, Britain. Marson is the Jamaican voice in this selection. The setting is on-air conversation; the surviving clip does not establish how much was prepared.

Listen in three passes

  1. The exchange: notice questions, responses and turn-taking.
  2. The voice: follow Marson’s phrasing and the way she addresses her guest. Avoid treating a broadcasting register as an absence of Jamaican identity.
  3. The setting: write one thing the recording reveals about the interaction and one thing you would need another source to establish.

FILM WITH SOUND · REPORTER/PASSENGER EXCHANGES · JUNE 1948

Windrush passengers answer a reporter

Where: Tilbury, England. British Pathé’s film includes short passenger interviews within its newsreel framing. The arrival sequence follows an unrelated opening; the player starts approximately 43 seconds into the publisher’s upload.

Give the reply its own space

  1. Follow one spoken reply before the song. What question prompted it?
  2. Separate the passenger’s answer from the reporter and the unseen commentator.
  3. Note a pause, repetition or emphasis you actually hear. Describe that moment rather than naming a supposed island-wide accent.
Why the song needs a different label

The interviewer’s exchange and the requested performance do different things. Lord Kitchener is Trinidadian, and singing organizes words differently from an ordinary reply. The film is a mixed source; the whole soundtrack should not be filed as natural Jamaican speech.

FILM WITH SOUND · STAGE-MANAGED MESSAGES · 1943

Hello! West Indies: speaking to home on film

Una Marson’s program gives this Ministry of Information film its format. The BFI describes these messages as stage-managed. Use it to explore public address and wartime framing; do not treat it as unobserved everyday conversation or assume every participant is Jamaican.

Watch for: who is addressed, the role of the microphone and how a personal message serves a public film. Compare it with Marson’s interviewer role in 1940.
