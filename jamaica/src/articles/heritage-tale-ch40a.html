The Rain.

A goat uses transformation and another animal’s help to escape.

← Written talesJamaican voices →

Teller and place: Richard Pottinger, Claremont, St. Ann.

Reading note: Threat of being eaten.

Edition: collected during Beckwith’s 1919/1921 visits; published in 1924. These historical transcriptions are separate from the modern performances in the listening room.

Anansi and Goat have a little quarrel. Anansi said to Goat, “Brar Goat, I gwine ketch you!” Goat say, “You never live, me frien’, to ketch me!” Goat ’fraid fe rain. So one moist night Goat was coming from his field had to pass Anansi’s house, drizzle drizzle rain fall; Brar Goat have to run up Anansi’s house.—“Come in, me frien’!” Goat go in. Anansi step in a room tak out him fiddle:—

“Me t’ank Brar Rain

Fe run wil’ meat from bush

Come a house.”

Goat didn’t like it, keep to de door-way. Anansi not notice him, only playing de same song. Goat jump down de bottom door Anansi cut after him. Goat can’t cross river, run to de river-side turn a white little stone. Dog see, de odder side of de river, when Goat turned a little stone. Anansi run up de river now.—“Brar Dog, see Brar Goat pass?”—“Yes, Brar! see one little stone a river-side deh? takee up t’rowee, I show you whe’ him deh.” Anansi tak up de stone, t’row it de odder side, give Brar Dog. Goat drop on him four feet. Anansi say, “Luck in me han’ an’ it get ’way!”

Look again

Who performs the decisive trick: Goat, Dog or Anansi?
