Frome, 1938: the factory, the wages and the official story

Westmoreland’s labour history begins with people seeking work, a factory under construction and a wage dispute. The documents let us examine how that conflict was reported.

A new factory did not guarantee a better life

Frome belongs in the history of industrial change as well as the history of protest. The National Library’s account on Flickr describes the consolidation of estates and the building of a central factory, attracting people looking for work. It places the conflict during construction, before the central factory’s opening in 1939. That sequence matters. A completed factory photograph cannot be treated as a picture of the wage confrontation itself. [2]

Follow the report day by day

On 4 May 1938, Colonial Secretary William Ormsby-Gore answered questions in the British Commons using a telegram from Jamaica’s governor. His account placed broken windows during wage payment on 29 April, a strike by construction labourers seeking higher wages the following day, and a confrontation at Old Frome on 2 May. It reported four dead, nine wounded and 93 arrests. These are the figures and dates in that official report, not a final reconciled casualty register. [1]

Listen to the language of authority

The reply described police firing as necessary and portrayed the disturbance as local and estate-specific. Those were judgments made by a colonial official reporting to Parliament. They should not silently become the guide’s own conclusion about the proportionality of force or the workers’ grievances. The questions around the reply raised wider labour conditions and the ability of workers to organise. The document therefore preserves disagreement, not only a sequence of events. [1]

The later account is not identical

NLJ’s Flickr description places the deadly clash on 3 May and gives thirteen wounded and more than one hundred arrests. It connects recruitment, inadequate conditions and wages below expectations with the wider labour unrest. We can identify the differences without pretending to resolve them. Contemporary reporting can be incomplete; a later summary can compress events or reproduce a different tally. Establishing a definitive chronology would require the enquiry papers, police records, newspapers and named victims’ records to be compared. [2]

From a Westmoreland dispute to public memory

The Library’s Labour Day history places Frome within the island’s 1938 struggles over wages and labour organisation. It dates the replacement of Empire Day by Labour Day to legislation in 1961. Do not jump directly from a wage payment in 1938 to independence as though every later development was inevitable. Keep the local dispute, the spread of organising and the later choice of public commemoration as related but distinct histories. [3]

Examine the factory image beside the documents

The NLJ photograph on Flickr offers an identified view of Frome’s industrial site. Open it and compare the scale of structures with the parliamentary language about a factory being erected. The image’s capture date is not established here; its upload date is not a historical date. Record what can actually be seen, then ask what is missing: pay arrangements, temporary lodging, recruitment and the experience of people who never secured a job.

A learning exercise that makes the disagreement useful

Create two columns headed “4 May parliamentary report” and “NLJ retrospective account.” Record each date and casualty figure with its source. Add a third column for the evidence needed to reconcile them. Finally, write a short explanation of how the accounts frame workers and police differently. That exercise gives Westmoreland’s history depth without turning uncertainty into a sensational story.

Tony’s Call Best first as a documentary learning session. If visiting the Frome area, arrange interpretation with a knowledgeable local host; a working or former industrial site is not an automatically open attraction. Allow 1–2 hours for an agreed local history visit, excluding transfers, as a planning estimate. Confirm permission, guide charges, parking, site conditions and accessible walking surfaces. Skip an unannounced factory entry.
