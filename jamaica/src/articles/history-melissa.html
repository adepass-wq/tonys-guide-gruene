Melissa in Falmouth: damaged institutions and the long work of recovery

Follow dated evidence from the October 2025 hurricane through emergency care, school disruption, household grants and later repair reports. Damage, funding and completed restoration are different findings.

A town is more than its famous buildings

Hurricane Melissa struck Jamaica on 28 October 2025. In Trelawny, the early municipal report identified destruction or severe damage affecting the municipal building, infirmary, Roads and Works department, relief offices, courthouse and fire station, with markets also affected. The national damage assessment separately documents Falmouth Courthouse’s heritage damage. [1] [2]

Those losses interrupt different parts of daily life. A damaged courthouse, school, market, health facility or home creates different needs and different repair paths. Describing the whole town as simply “destroyed” or “recovered” obscures that work. A visitor needs the specific place, service and date.

The hospital assessment had a specific denominator

PAHO/WHO’s 11 November 2025 report assessed approximately 60–70 percent roof loss at Falmouth Hospital. It identified roof losses affecting surgical and paediatric wards and A&E, leakage at maternity and destroyed dietary/storage areas. That percentage concerns the hospital, not the proportion of Falmouth’s buildings destroyed. [3]

It is an important example of reading a number properly. The damaged facility, the assessed component and the report date belong with the statistic. Otherwise, a precise-looking figure can be made to tell a story the source never supported.

Emergency care while renovation was being planned

By the 14 November ministerial visit, Spain’s field hospital on the grounds was treating patients. JIS reported plans to begin renovation the following week, prioritizing wards and key clinical services. Emergency provision and an announced construction sequence were milestones; neither meant the whole hospital was restored. [4]

School damage changed the shape of learning

William Knibb Memorial High School’s principal reported seven buildings damaged, six severely, in November 2025. A shift system was being considered, dependent on utilities and safety. The report describes a proposal under disrupted conditions, not proof that the proposed timetable had already begun. [5]

A roof affects more than the appearance of a building. Learning space, equipment, timetables and the daily journeys of students and staff can all be disrupted. The photograph below lets you examine documented damage at a named institution instead of substituting a generic storm image.

Household recovery and later hospital work

A March 2026 JIS report recorded J$366 million in ROOFS grants for affected Trelawny families, with the handover at Bounty Hall on 11 March. This is evidence of a parish household-support milestone. It is not an audit establishing that every recipient’s house had been repaired. [6]

The recovery timeline continued

In September 2026, reporting based on an interview with the hospital CEO described a J$520 million rehabilitation project and specific A&E improvements. That is later, facility-specific evidence. It still does not establish the October operating condition of every ward or all the town’s other services. [7]

The national MSME restoration programme opened applications on 6 October 2026. Its support for premises, equipment and tools adds another part of the recovery picture: the ability of businesses to work again. A national programme cannot be used as a Falmouth completion percentage. [8]

What a responsible visit should do

Confirm a building’s actual opening and the route to it. Respect barriers, repairs, active school premises and health facilities. A photograph taken after the storm may show only part of a structure; funding or a start announcement is not a completion certificate.

Spend deliberately with identifiable local businesses that are operating, and arrange a guide who can explain the town without taking you through unsafe premises. Ask before photographing residents or damaged homes. Recovery is people’s continuing life and work, not a spectacle for a visitor’s camera.

This account is checked to 12 October 2026. It establishes documented damage and dated milestones; no town-wide completion audit is claimed. Use current official and operator information for a specific visit.
