The Golden Cage.

A king’s daughter and a bird’s warning song.

← Written talesJamaican voices →

Teller and place: William Harris, Maggotty.

Reading note: A child is thrown into a well; retaliatory violence.

Edition: collected during Beckwith’s 1919/1921 visits; published 1924. Historical transcription, not a newly composed retelling. Narrative wording and printed song words retained; page and reference markers removed. This text is not a transcript of the modern performances in the listening room.

A king had a daughter. He had two servants who did not like the daughter. One day the two servants were going to the well for water and the daughter said she wanted to go with them. And they catch the little girl and cast her in the well. Three days after, the little girl went home to her father an’ the father catch the two servants and throw them in the well. And he get his child and thus end the story.

Cheep, cheep, cheep, cheep. I brought a news to tell you. Cheep, cheep, cheep, cheep. I brought a news to tell you. Miss Chee Chee take you, one dear love an’ cast her in-to a well. Be qui-et, be qui-et, I will make a gold-en cage an’ put you in-to it. No, no, no, no. Same me will do it to dear love too, you will do with me the same.
