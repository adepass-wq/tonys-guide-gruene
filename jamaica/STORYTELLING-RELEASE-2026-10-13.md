# Jamaican storytelling and African connections

The storytelling collection contains 24 historical tale entries, five recordings by identified Jamaican performers, three African video resources and three contextual articles. A chapter with two printed variants keeps their individual teller headings; the fasting-trial fragment is explicitly labelled. These are 24 entries, not 24 newly made recordings.

The written entries preserve narrative and song wording from selected material in Martha Warren Beckwith's Jamaica Anansi Stories (1924), collected in 1919 and 1921 with music recorded by Helen Roberts. Every selected source paragraph was compared against the imported reader output. Page interruptions and reference markers are removed; printed editorial footnotes remain available through exact source-entry links. The selections contain approximately 5,837 source words. New summaries and questions are distinguished from historical texts.

Jamaican performances:

- Louise Bennett-Coverley: Anancy and Smoked Pork; Dinky and Anancy & Yellow Snake; Jamaican Proverbs. National Library publication and biography: https://nlj.gov.jm/exhibition/misslou/
- Amina Blackwood Meeks: The Wisdom Calabash, CALIFEST 2020 / CARICON. Performer biography and JIS reports establish Jamaican storytelling context.
- Winsome Monica Minott: a chapter from Travels of Ananse, Kaori Nagai / Rethinking Fables. The author's first-person account describes growing up hearing stories in Jamaica. Explicitly a modern adaptation.

African resources:

- Kwaku Ananse and the Pot of Wisdom (Twi), published by Kwabena Antwi-Boasiako. Speaker not separately credited; uploader not silently assigned the voice. A source-provided English caption track is available.
- Kweku Ananse and the Chest of Stories, production near Kakum, Ghana, 2013, filmmaker's 2016 upload. Film adaptation.
- Who is Kweku Ananse?, part 1, filmmaker's 2012 upload. Producer identifies Twifu Hemang-area community speakers including farmers, a herbalist, a paramount chief, former nurses and schoolchildren. Interviewers are identified separately.
- Reading route to Ghanaian-born author Adwoa Badoe and Malian illustrator Baba Wagué Diakité through the publisher's excerpt for The Pot of Wisdom.
