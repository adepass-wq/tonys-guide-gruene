## Ackee and saltfish — two independently presented preparations
1. [Jamaica Tourist Board, chefs at Hyatt Resorts Montego Bay](https://www.visitjamaica.com/blog/post/ackee-and-saltfish-in-jamaica/) (18 December 2024): one pound saltfish, fresh ackees or one can, onion, bell pepper, two tomatoes, garlic, thyme, pepper, two tablespoons oil; cooking class in Montego Bay is specifically attributed.
2. [Jamaica Travel and Culture, Tracy's illustrated/video recipe](https://jamaicatravelandculture.com/food_and_drink/ackee_and_saltfish.htm) (site copyright 2006; recipe publication date not individually confirmed): half-pound saltfish, a drained can of ackee or 12 fresh ackees, onion, sweet pepper, one tomato, thyme, butter instead of oil; garlic, scallion, and bacon are listed optional.

**Interpretation:** These independently published recipes agree on ackee, saltfish and aromatics but differ substantially in fish quantity, oil/butter and optional ingredients. Neither formula authorizes us to invent a single canonical Jamaican proportion. The second source's video playback and cook identity beyond 'Tracy' have not been independently verified. Any site summary should link readers to both sources and credit their methods distinctly.

## Rice and peas — three distinct published formulations
1. [Jamaica Travel and Culture, Tracy](https://jamaicatravelandculture.com/food_and_drink/rice_and_peas.htm): three cups rice, kidney/gungo peas, garlic, coconut milk, scallion, thyme and uncut pepper. The page also describes cooking dried peas before combining rice.
2. [Gabi Odebode for Food Network Kitchen](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/jamaican-rice-and-peas-19549684): a clearly attributed modern preparation noting kidney beans and gungo peas as variants, with coconut, scallions, pimento and thyme. This source is supplementary context, not proof of Jamaican residence or community fieldwork.
3. [Jamaican Cookery, fresh local red peas version](https://www.jamaicancookery.com/2026/01/authentic-jamaican-rice-and-peas-local.html) (January 2026): fresh local red peas, Alberta parboiled rice, freshly prepared coconut milk and named aromatics.

**Interpretation:** Bean type, rice variety, and coconut source are documented variation axes. Original Tony's Guide dried-bean quantities and cooking times still lack provenance. Do not infer a parish-wide practice from a single online cook. Cooking dried red kidney beans requires a separate validated safety check; never adopt vague boiling guidance by inference.
