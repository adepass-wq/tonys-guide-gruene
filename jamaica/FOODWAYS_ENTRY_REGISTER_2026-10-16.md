# Jamaican Foodways: 107-entry documentary register

Review date: 16 October 2026. Staging only. No production release authorized.

**Scope:** Every original entry is retained for accountability. These are 107 records, including aliases and serving combinations, not 107 independently documented traditions. Each source was opened except the explicitly failed candidate. A published recipe authenticates the existence of that publisher’s preparation; it does not establish the original Tony’s Guide draft, an origin story or kitchen performance.

**Across all 107:** original formula withheld and unverified; no recorded Tony’s Guide kitchen test; no historical origin inferred from the contemporary recipe. Full ingredient quantities and instructions remain at the publisher. No copyrighted recipe photography is reproduced.

## Further corroboration and research limits

- Joan Lue’s recipe biography identifies her as a Jamaican chef and cookbook author. Noel Cunningham’s peppered-shrimp article identifies his Jamaican-Canadian background and Kingston upbringing. Sian Rose’s turn-cornmeal account explicitly locates her own childhood in western Jamaica. These are attributed accounts, not independent parish-wide ethnography.
- Cook Like a Jamaican corroborates many dish names, but inspected pages include inconsistent unit conversions. The dumpling page also exposed an unsolicited verification link in user review text. That text was ignored and the visitor-facing dumpling and banana-fritter references were replaced with another publisher.
- A pseudonymous Jamaicans.com coco-bread submission specifies cocoa powder and loaf baking. It was rejected as the reference for folded coco bread; Tanya Harris’s published preparation was selected instead.
- The roundup used to document stew pork contains an erroneous seventeenth-century indenture claim and confuses broad beans with gungo peas. Only its identification of stew pork is used, not that historical chronology or bean equivalence.
- The National Library of Jamaica overview contains useful named sections but also broad, dated generalizations. It is not used as authority for a modern formula or a complete chronology of crop introductions. The prior claim that it documents Ital practice could not be substantiated and was removed.
- Cook-down ackee and callaloo is indexed in Craig and Shaun McAnuff’s *Original Flava: Caribbean Recipes from Home*, p. 142, at https://www.eatyourbooks.com/library/recipes/2434326/cook-down-ackee-and-callaloo . This is a useful bibliographic lead; the full page and formula have not been inspected. It therefore does not close entry 76’s recipe-provenance gate.
- A PBC Jamaica June-plum chutney video was located at https://www.facebook.com/pbcjamaica/videos/june-plum-chutney/294497734525043/ . The full preparation and speaker were not inspected. The Gleaner candidate failed text decoding. Entry 106 remains unresolved.
- Historical recipe authentication is not exhaustive: commercial cookbook full text, restricted archives, oral interviews and kitchen trials were not available in this session. A failure to establish a preparation in this research does not establish nonexistence.

## Kitchen-validation handoff

For each formula proposed for local publication: obtain the actual published ingredient list and method; record the edition/page or source revision; list every substitution and conversion; resolve source omissions with the author; have a competent cook prepare the exact version; record weights, yield, equipment, actual temperatures and times, texture, taste, photographs and problems; repeat after corrections. Independently review ackee, cassava, dried kidney beans, meat and seafood, allergens, alcohol, and preservation. Do not certify a shelf life or canning process from a family anecdote.
